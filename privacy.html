Privacy Policy

Effective 22 September 2026

The short version

  • You sign in with Google. Almanac receives your name, email address and profile picture from Google.
  • Your tasks, lists, notes and habits are saved to your account so they follow you to another phone.
  • Vault notes, and the photos and files you attach, never leave your phone.
  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers. Nothing is sold or shared for marketing.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from inside the app or from this page.

Almanac is a to-do app made by Starry Works ("we"). This policy explains what the app stores, where it is stored, who can reach it and how to get rid of it. It covers the Almanac Android app and this website.

1. Signing in

Almanac needs an account, and Google is the only way to sign in. When you sign in, Google gives the app your name, email address, profile picture and your Google account ID. That is all we receive. We never see your Google password, and Almanac asks Google for nothing else: no contacts, no calendar, no files, no Gmail.

Your account exists so your own content can be stored and sent back to you. It is not used for marketing, profiling or advertising.

2. What is saved to your account

These are saved to your account and sent to your other phones when you sign in on them:

  • Tasks: title, notes, due date and time, reminder, repeat, priority, whether and when you completed it, and when you created it.
  • Lists: name, colour, icon and order.
  • Subtasks: title and whether they are done.
  • Notes and folders, except anything in the vault.
  • Habits: name, colour, icon, schedule, and the days you checked off.

This is whatever you type into the app. If you would rather something was not stored on a server, keep it in the vault or don't put it in Almanac.

3. What stays on your phone

These are never uploaded, and they do not move to another phone:

  • Vault notes and vault folders. Moving a note into the vault also removes the copy that was in your account.
  • Attachments: every photo and file you attach to a task.
  • Your vault PIN, which is kept only as a scrambled (hashed) value on the phone and can't be read back, by us or anyone else.
  • Settings: which features you turned on, haptics, your daily reminder and default list.

4. Who can see it

Your content is stored so that only your signed-in account can read it. The database refuses requests for anyone else's rows. It is not published, not shared with other users, not sold, and not given to advertisers or data brokers. There are no ads, analytics or tracking tools in the app.

Two companies are involved in running the app:

  • Google handles the sign-in, and Google Play distributes the app.
  • Supabase hosts the database your content is stored in.

As the person who operates that database, the developer has administrative access to it. Your content is not read except when you ask for help with a specific problem and we cannot solve it any other way. We will also hand over data if the law requires it.

5. Where it is stored, and for how long

The database runs on Supabase servers in Singapore. Your content is sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) and is encrypted on disk by Supabase.

It is kept until you delete it. There is no automatic expiry: an account that is never used is not cleared out on its own, so delete it if you want it gone.

6. Deleting things

A single task, note or habit

Deleting one in the app removes its contents from your account as well. A small marker is kept, holding only that item's random id and the fact that it was deleted, so your other phones know to remove their copy too. It contains nothing you typed.

Everything on this phone

Settings › Delete all data clears the app on the phone, including vault notes and attachments, and that deletion also travels to your account.

Your whole account

Settings › Account › Delete account erases the account and everything stored in it, markers included. See the deletion page for what happens and for how to ask if you no longer have the app.

7. Permissions

Almanac asks for these only when you use the feature behind them:

  • Camera: to take a photo and attach it to a task.
  • Photos and media: to attach a photo you already have.
  • Files: to attach a file, or to import a backup you picked.
  • Notifications: to show the reminders you set.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock: to open your vault. The check is done by Android; Almanac never receives your fingerprint or face data.

8. Backups and export

Supabase keeps operational backups of the database, which are deleted on its own schedule. On your phone, Android's own backup may include Almanac's data, depending on your device settings; that is handled by Google, not by us.

Settings › Export tasks writes your tasks and lists to a file and hands it to whichever app you choose. Where it goes after that is up to you.

9. Children

Almanac is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly create accounts for them. If you believe a child has signed in, write to us and we will delete the account.

10. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with a new effective date. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the app's release notes on Google Play.
